Welcome

Moving your data-center overlay from VXLAN to SRv6 — the comparison and the playbook.

EVPN-VXLAN is mature, widely deployed, and well understood. EVPN-SRv6 is where the silicon and toolchain investment is heading. This guide covers both the technical delta between the two encapsulations and the step-by-step mechanics of migrating a live fabric without a maintenance window.

Who this is for

  • Network operators running a production EVPN-VXLAN leaf-spine fabric who need a concrete migration path to SRv6.
  • DC architects evaluating VXLAN vs. SRv6 encapsulation for a new build or a next-generation overlay design.

The Migration Question

Why move a perfectly working VXLAN fabric to SRv6?

VXLAN works. If your fabric is stable and hitting no hard limits, "because SRv6 is newer" is not an engineering justification. This section lays out the real reasons operators migrate — and the equally important reasons not to.

Header tax and scale

  • VXLAN carries an 8-byte VXLAN header plus a full UDP/IP outer — 50 bytes of overhead per packet before the inner Ethernet frame.
  • SRv6 with a uSID-only path (no SRH in the data plane) adds only a 40-byte IPv6 header; the entire forwarding instruction is encoded inside the DA.
  • VXLAN VNI is 24 bits — 16 million identifiers. SRv6 SID space is 128 bits; identifier exhaustion is not a practical concern at any fabric scale.
  • uSID compression means multiple segment instructions fit in a single 128-bit address, keeping MTU impact flat even for multi-hop TE paths.

Unifying SP + DC underlay

  • If your DC connects to a service-provider or WAN backbone that already runs SRv6, a SRv6 DC overlay lets the same IPv6/SRv6 underlay span both domains — no encap boundary at the DC edge.
  • One technology means one skill set, one set of tooling (path computation, telemetry collectors, verification scripts), and one operational model across campus, DC, and WAN.
  • End-to-end SRv6 eliminates the VXLAN-to-MPLS (or VXLAN-to-SRv6) hand-off stitching that today lives on a dedicated border or DCI node.

Service insertion and traffic engineering

  • SR Policy and Flex-Algo are first-class constructs in SRv6 — low-latency paths, disjoint paths, and color-based steering are native.
  • Bolting TE onto a VXLAN fabric requires overlay tricks (VXLAN policy maps, DSCP remarking) that are brittle and hard to verify end-to-end.
  • SRv6 service function chaining (SFC) inserts middleboxes by placing their SID in the segment list — no bump-in-the-wire VLAN gymnastics.

Vendor and silicon roadmap pressure

  • New ASIC generations (Cisco Silicon One, Broadcom Jericho3-AI, Marvell Teralynx 10) are SRv6-first; VXLAN feature parity on the latest pipelines is increasingly an afterthought.
  • Some vendor product lines have already sunset VXLAN feature development; bug fixes continue but new capabilities (Enhanced EVPN, per-flow TE) land on SRv6 first.
  • If your refresh cycle aligns with the next ASIC generation, designing for SRv6 now avoids a second migration later.

What's NOT a reason to migrate

  • Your fabric is pure DC with no SP/WAN integration story — the "unified underlay" argument doesn't apply, and VXLAN operational simplicity may outweigh any benefit.
  • You are at moderate scale (sub-10k VNIs, no cross-DC TE requirements) and VXLAN is meeting all SLAs — the overhead delta is immaterial.
  • Your operations team has deep VXLAN expertise and no SRv6 familiarity — a forced migration under time pressure is a risk, not an improvement.
  • If any of these apply, read §21 Decision Matrix before proceeding.
